Prevention & Technique

Why most people brush wrong — and what actually works

K
Prof. Dr. Krithika Datta, MDS, Dip.IBE Lead Practitioner & Founder
May 14, 2026 · 5 min read

For something we do twice a day, brushing teeth is surprisingly misunderstood. Most adults brush too hard, move in quick horizontal saw-like strokes, or stop after less than 45 seconds. Over time, these habits cause tooth sensitivity, enamel abrasion, and receding gums.

Key Takeaways

  • Angle matters: Position bristles at a 45-degree angle toward the gum line.
  • Light pressure: You are sweeping soft plaque, not scrubbing grout.
  • Timing: Two full minutes is essential for fluoride absorption and full coverage.

1. The 45-Degree Angle Technique (Modified Bass Method)

Plaque collects most heavily along the crevice where your tooth meets your gum (the gingival margin). Holding your toothbrush flat against your teeth misses this critical zone entirely.

Instead, tilt your brush head at a 45-degree angle so the bristles point gently toward your gums. Use short, gentle circular or flicking motions away from the gumline. This gently massages the gums while sweeping away sub-gingival debris.

2. Stop Scrubbing: The Danger of Enamel Abrasion

Hard scrubbing wears down protective tooth enamel and causes gum recession, exposing sensitive dentin underneath. If your toothbrush bristles flare outward within a few weeks of use, you are using far too much pressure.

"Think of brushing like polishing expensive crystal glass — gentle, deliberate, and smooth. Excessive force does not make teeth cleaner; it strips away your enamel."

3. The 2-Minute Rule & Quad System

Divide your mouth into four quadrants: upper right, upper left, lower left, and lower right. Spend a full 30 seconds on each quadrant, covering inner surfaces, outer surfaces, and chewing faces.

4. Manual vs. Electric: What Science Says

While a manual brush can be completely effective with impeccable technique, sonic electric toothbrushes remove significantly more plaque with built-in pressure sensors that prevent over-brushing.
